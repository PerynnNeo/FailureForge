# Free-end stiffness of NIST reference cantilever W2 30 D

Use the 600 µm beam on die W2 30 D. Compare the model's free-end transverse stiffness with the electrostatic force balance (EFB) measurement for this member in Table 1 of NIST SP 260-227. This is a retrospective comparison with a previously published physical experiment. Freeze the prediction before transcribing the selected result. A footnote for the other die, W2 46 A, was visible during source review. Its measurements are excluded from this trial. [NIST certification study, sections 2, 3, 6 and Appendix C](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.SP.260-227)

Use the published nominal geometry and estimated final device thickness. Assume an ideal clamp, a uniform rectangular section and the effective bending modulus of 169.8 GPa reported for the earlier silicon reference-cantilever design. The selected specimen's actual thickness, clamp compliance and crystal direction have not been measured separately here. Set the numerical tip load to 1 nN and evaluate the ratio of load to free-end deflection in the linear regime. The NIST observable is stiffness in N/m. The calculation does not require the instrument to apply this numerical probe force. [NIST precursor design, equations 1 and 3](https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=850947)

Record the EFB central value and absolute uncertainty directly from the named row. Compare the point prediction to the reported interval and retain its stated uncertainty basis. Leave model uncertainty unquantified. Preserve any disagreement before investigating alternative geometry, material or fixture assumptions.
